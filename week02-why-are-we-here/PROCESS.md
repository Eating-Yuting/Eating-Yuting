# Process

*How I wrote this essay, and how I used AI.*

## What this essay is built on

Everything in the essay comes from things I actually did in Week 1:

- running and modifying the *Schotter* sketch (changing SEED, CHAOS,
  colours), making the heart version, and building a 3D "chocolate bar"
  version with AI's help;
- my first `git push` failing with 403, a wrong branch name
  (`origin/main` vs `2026`), a leftover `index.lock`, and a garbled
  character that broke my program;
- watching Dylan Beattie's *The Art of Code* and writing down the three
  moments that impressed me (code as song lyrics, the little bird game,
  patterns that grow like living things).

## How I wrote it

1. After the exercises and the film, I wrote my own notes — seven plain
   English lines ("change some numbers / change to the heart / ...").
   These lines became the backbone of the essay and are quoted in it.
2. I told an AI assistant my seven-line outline, my impressions of the
   film, and my bug stories, and asked it to help draft the essay in
   English around my outline.
3. I read the draft, corrected details, decided what to keep and what to
   cut, and added my own points (why designers need to *read* code, and
   how much is enough).
4. I committed each stage separately, so the git history shows how the
   essay developed from draft to final version.

## How AI was used

- AI was used for: discussing ideas, drafting English text from my
  outline and notes, polishing grammar, and answering my git / Python
  questions while I did the exercises.
- AI was NOT used for: the experiences, opinions, and the seven-line
  outline — these are mine. The exercise code (heart version, chocolate
  version) was built by me with AI assistance, and the committing,
  pushing, and debugging were done by me.

## What I learned from writing this way

Using AI honestly made me think harder, not less: AI can produce text
fast, but it cannot decide what *I* want to say. The seven lines came
from my week; the essay just explains them.
